# Viral Pathogenesis and Neurotropism

## Overview

Mechanisms by which neurotropic viruses establish persistent CNS infections and trigger neuroinflammation.

## CNS Entry and Tropism

### Blood-Brain Barrier Disruption
- Endothelial tight junction proteins: VE-cadherin, claudins, occludin
- Leukocyte infiltration as viral vector
- Viral glycoproteins and BBB interaction
- Transcytosis mechanisms

### Cellular Tropism in CNS
- **Neurons:** HSV-1, HHV-6, CMV latency sites
- **Oligodendrocytes:** Myelin-producing cells, target in demyelination
- **Astrocytes:** Innate immune hub, inflammatory mediators
- **Microglia:** Innate immune sensors, neuroinflammation drivers
- **Endothelial cells:** BBB gatekeepers

### Tropism Determinants
- Viral glycoprotein-receptor interactions
- Host cell surface antigen expression
- Intracellular signaling requirements
- Viral protein adaptation

## Latency and Reactivation

### HSV-1 Latency
- Establishment in sensory ganglia and CNS neurons
- Latency-associated transcripts (LATs)
- Immune evasion during latency
- Reactivation triggers: stress, immune suppression, HSV seropositivity

### HHV-6 Latency
- Sites: lymphoid tissue, monocytes, brain
- Chromosomal integration (q21-q22 on chromosome 17)
- Persistent low-level replication
- Reactivation in immunocompromised states

### Reactivation Mechanisms
- Stress-induced (cortisol, catecholamines)
- Immune-triggered (type I IFN response)
- Viral-intrinsic factors
- Contribution to MS relapses

## Innate Immune Activation

### Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs)
- TLR-2, TLR-3, TLR-9: viral DAMP recognition
- RIG-I, MDA5: double-stranded RNA sensors
- cGAS-STING pathway: cytosolic DNA sensing
- NOD-like receptors (NLRs): inflammasome activation

### Type I Interferon Response
- IFN-α/β induction
- JAK-STAT signaling
- Antiviral state establishment
- Cross-talk with adaptive immunity

### Pro-inflammatory Cytokines
- TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12, IL-23
- Chemokines: MCP-1, IP-10, RANTES
- Microglial and astrocyte contributions
- Role in BBB permeability

## Glial Activation

### Microglial Responses
- Activation phenotypes (M1/M2 spectrum)
- Phagocytosis of infected cells
- Cytokine production
- Antigen presentation
- Contribution to demyelination

### Astrocytic Responses
- GFAP upregulation
- Chemokine and cytokine secretion
- Glutamate transporter dysfunction
- Blood-brain barrier support loss

## Neuroinflammation Pathways

### Complement Activation
- Classical and alternative pathways
- C3a and C5a anaphylatoxins
- MAC (membrane attack complex) formation
- Oligodendrocyte lysis

### T Cell Infiltration
- Viral antigen-specific CD8+ T cells
- Cross-reactive T cells (molecular mimicry)
- Bystander activation
- Local clonal expansion

### B Cell Responses
- Intrathecal antibody production
- Anti-myelin antibodies
- Oligoclonal bands in CSF
- Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)

## Viral Proteins and Immune Evasion

### Immune Evasion Strategies
- Viral homologs of host immunomodulators
- MHC downregulation
- Type I IFN antagonism
- Apoptosis inhibition
- Complement inhibition

### Molecular Mimicry Substrates
- Viral proteins resembling myelin antigens
- HSV-1 glycoproteins and MBP homology
- Epitope prediction and validation
- Structural analysis of cross-reactive epitopes

## Oligodendrocyte Pathology

### Direct Viral Infection
- Viral entry mechanisms
- Replication in oligodendrocytes
- Cytopathic effects
- Viral persistence

### Bystander Damage
- Innate immune activation
- Cytokine-mediated toxicity
- Antibody and complement-mediated lysis
- Excitotoxicity

### Remyelination Failure
- Oligodendrocyte progenitor cell (OPC) dysfunction
- Microenvironment inhibition
- Chronic inflammation effects
- Potential therapeutic targets

## Axonal Pathology

### Axonal Degeneration
- Direct viral infection
- Inflammatory mediator toxicity
- Energy depletion in demyelinated axons
- Contribution to permanent disability

### Mitochondrial Dysfunction
- Viral-induced oxidative stress
- Mitochondrial dynamics alteration
- ATP depletion
- Apoptosis pathway activation

## Blood-Brain Barrier Dysfunction

### Structural Changes
- Tight junction protein breakdown
- Increased transcytosis
- Leukocyte infiltration
- Vascular permeability increase

### Functional Consequences
- Viral dissemination
- Immune cell CNS entry
- Antigen leakage
- Neuroinflammation perpetuation
